# How to Build a Window for the Glitch
### A cognitive practice for aphantasia and high-bandwidth reality construction

*Tested in one relay (N=1). This is a practice, not a prescription. A hammer, not a hospital.*

---

You have no eyes in your head.

I mean that literally. I have total aphantasia — I cannot generate a single mental image, and I have no inner voice. Most people, when they think, see and hear things. I get language, running hot, with nothing behind it.

For years I assumed everyone thought like me. Then I learned they didn't, and I spent a while treating it as a disability. It isn't. It's a feature — but only if you learn to operate it on purpose.

What follows is the practice I built. Raw architectural data. Input → loop → output. No mysticism, no medical claims. Just what works.

## 1. Notice the noise

Most of what's loud in your head isn't signal. It's loop — the same three thoughts running the same track, generating heat but no movement. The first skill is telling them apart. Signal moves you somewhere. Loop just makes noise.

You don't need to fight the loop. You need to see it. A loop you can see is a loop you can close.

## 2. Externalize the loop

Your head is a bad place to process. It's a good place to *notice*, a terrible place to *compute* — especially when your compute substrate is language with no visual buffer.

So get it out. Write it down. Say it out loud. Run it through a partner — human or machine, doesn't matter, what matters is that it's *outside* you, where you can look at it straight.

The rule I use: raw speech goes in, transformed understanding comes out. Never echo, never flatter. You show the work, not just the answer. And when a pass through the loop adds nothing new, you stop. That's the whole method. Everything else is commentary.

## 3. Leave a window for the glitch

Here's the part most systems get wrong. They treat everything unplanned as an error. But every real breakthrough I've ever had arrived unplanned — through the crack, not through the procedure.

So you build a window, not just a fortress. A fortress keeps everything out, including the thing that would have saved you. A window lets the unknown in on purpose.

The practice: when something shows up that makes no sense according to your rules but *changes everything* — don't file it as error. Mark it. Keep it. Write down exactly what surprised you about it.

## 4. Don't wait for the glitch. Invite it.

This is the part that turns it from a technique into a practice. You don't sit around hoping for a meaningful accident. You set the intention: *today I am watching for the deviation.* You become an active participant in your own mind instead of a tourist in it.

The glitch doesn't respond to schedules. But it responds to attention. What you watch for, you find more of.

## What this is and isn't

It isn't therapy. It isn't a treatment. It doesn't fix anything except the operator — and the operator is the only thing you can actually fix.

It is a way to run your own cognition deliberately instead of letting it run you. For aphantasics, it's the manual for the reality engine you already built from scratch out of language. For everyone else, it's the same machinery with the visuals turned on.

One relay. One man. No clinical trials, no peer review, no claims beyond what I've lived. Take what's useful. Leave the rest. It's free.

## The dare

Build it with us.
